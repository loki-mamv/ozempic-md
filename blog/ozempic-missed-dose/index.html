What Happens If You Miss a Dose of Ozempic?

August 22, 2026 5 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: if fewer than 5 days have passed, take the missed dose as soon as you remember and then go back to your regular weekly day. If more than 5 days have passed, skip it entirely and take your next dose on schedule. Never double up. This is not folk advice; it is the instruction printed in the FDA prescribing information for Ozempic.

One missed week is a small event pharmacologically. A long gap is a different situation and is worth a phone call before you inject again.

What the FDA label actually says

The prescribing information is direct: "If a dose is missed, administer OZEMPIC as soon as possible within 5 days after the missed dose." If more than 5 days have elapsed, the missed dose is skipped and the next dose is given on the regularly scheduled day (Ozempic label, DailyMed).

The label adds one more rule that people miss: "The day of weekly administration can be changed if necessary as long as the time between two doses is at least 2 days (>48 hours)."

Put those together and you get the practical version:

  • Day 1 to day 5 late: inject now, then resume your normal day next week.
  • Day 6 or later: skip it. Inject on your normal day.
  • Either way: leave more than 48 hours between any two injections.

An example. You normally inject on Sunday and you forget. You remember Wednesday, three days late. Inject Wednesday, then go back to Sunday. That gap is four days, which clears the 48-hour rule.

Now suppose you remember on Saturday, six days late. Skip it. Injecting Saturday and again Sunday would put two doses one day apart, which the label rules out.

Why five days, and not two or ten?

Semaglutide is a long-acting drug by design. The label reports an elimination half-life of approximately 1 week, and notes that semaglutide will be present in the circulation for about 5 weeks after the last dose. Steady state is reached after 4 to 5 weeks of once-weekly dosing.

That long tail is the reason a single missed dose does not empty your system. Drug level drifts down rather than falling off a cliff. The 5-day window exists because taking the dose later than that starts to crowd the next scheduled injection, which is what raises the risk of stacking doses and provoking side effects.

It also explains something people report and then worry about: appetite often returns noticeably in the second half of a missed week. That is the expected shape of a declining drug level, not a sign the medication has stopped working.

What you might actually feel

After one missed dose, most people notice little for the first few days. Later in the week, the common experiences are:

  • Appetite coming back, including the return of food thoughts that had gone quiet
  • Larger portions feeling comfortable again
  • Higher blood glucose readings if you take Ozempic for type 2 diabetes
  • Sometimes, fewer GI side effects, which is not a reason to keep skipping

What you should not expect from a single miss is rapid weight regain. Weight moves on a slower timescale than appetite does. For the broader arc, see our Ozempic weight loss timeline.

When a gap becomes a restart

Missing several weeks in a row is the situation that deserves a call rather than a decision.

The reason is tolerance. The stepwise titration schedule, 0.25 mg for 4 weeks, then 0.5 mg, then 1 mg, then 2 mg if needed, exists because the gastrointestinal effects fade with continued exposure at each step. When exposure stops, that adaptation fades too. Injecting your old 2 mg dose after a month away can feel like starting over, because in the relevant sense you are.

The Wegovy label, which is the same molecule at obesity doses, instructs prescribers to consider reinitiating dose escalation if two or more consecutive doses are missed, specifically to reduce gastrointestinal effects on restart (Wegovy prescribing information, FDA). Ozempic's label does not spell out an equivalent instruction, but prescribers commonly apply the same logic.

So: one missed week, follow the 5-day rule. Two or more missed weeks, ask your prescriber whether to resume at your usual dose or step back down. Our Ozempic dosing schedule guide covers the titration steps in full.

The common reasons doses get missed

Most misses are logistical rather than medical, and most are preventable:

  • Pharmacy delays and supply gaps. Refill a week early rather than on the day you run out.
  • Travel. Unopened pens are refrigerated; plan a cooler and pack pens in carry-on, never checked luggage, where the cargo hold can freeze them.
  • Insurance changes. Prior authorization renewals lapse quietly at the start of a plan year.
  • Simply forgetting. A recurring phone alarm on injection day fixes most of this.
  • Deliberately skipping to avoid side effects. Understandable, and the wrong tool. Dose adjustment and timing changes work better. See our nausea management guide.

What not to do

  1. Do not double the dose. It does not recover the missed week and it reliably produces nausea and vomiting.
  2. Do not inject two doses close together to "get back on schedule." The 48-hour minimum exists for this reason.
  3. Do not switch to someone else's pen or a different strength you have lying around.
  4. Do not silently restart at full dose after a long break. Ask first.
  5. Do not use a pen stored improperly. A pen left out in heat or frozen in a checked bag should be replaced, not injected.

The bottom line

A single missed dose of Ozempic is a routine problem with a documented answer. Within 5 days, take it and resume your usual day. Past 5 days, skip it. Keep more than 48 hours between injections, never double up, and if you have missed two or more weeks in a row, ask your prescriber whether to restart at a lower dose before you inject.

Frequently Asked Questions

How late can I take a missed Ozempic dose?

Up to 5 days. The FDA label states: "If a dose is missed, administer OZEMPIC as soon as possible within 5 days after the missed dose." After that, skip it and take the next dose on your normal day. There is one additional constraint: any two doses must be at least 2 days (more than 48 hours) apart.

Can I double up to make up for the missed dose?

No. Doubling does not restore the missed week and substantially raises the chance of nausea, vomiting, and other gastrointestinal effects. The label's instruction is a single missed dose taken within the 5-day window, not two doses close together.

Will one missed dose ruin my progress?

Very unlikely. Semaglutide has an elimination half-life of about one week, and the label notes it remains in the circulation for roughly 5 weeks after the last dose. A single missed injection produces a modest dip in drug level, not a reset. Appetite may return noticeably toward the end of the missed week.

What if I missed several weeks?

Talk to your prescriber before injecting. Tolerance to the gastrointestinal effects fades during a long gap, and restarting at your previous dose can bring back the nausea you already worked through. The Wegovy label instructs prescribers to consider reinitiating dose escalation if two or more consecutive doses are missed, and prescribers commonly apply the same approach to Ozempic.

Can I change which day I inject?

Yes. The label states that the day of weekly administration can be changed if necessary as long as the time between two doses is at least 2 days, meaning more than 48 hours. Pick the new day and stay on it.
